Right now, you know roughly what time it is. Probably to the minute. Your phone knows, your laptop knows, your microwave knows. And somewhere today, a number on a screen will decide when you eat, when you leave, and when you're late.

Now imagine a world where not one person on Earth knows what time it is. Not because they lost their watch — because the hour hasn't been invented yet. No minutes. No seconds. No "half past." No "running late."

That was the human condition for almost our entire existence. So how did people organise a day — or a year — with no clock at all? And how did we go from that world to one where a clock decides when you work?

The answer starts somewhere you'd never think to look: inside your own body.
